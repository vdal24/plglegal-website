## Mercedes-Benz Owners

A Mercedes-Benz should not spend more time with the service advisor than with you. If your C-Class, E-Class, S-Class, GLC, GLE, GLS, G-Class, AMG, EQB, EQE, EQS, or Sprinter keeps returning with the same defect, California's lemon law may require Mercedes-Benz USA to repurchase or replace it and pay your attorney fees.

## Mercedes-Benz Problems Owners Commonly Report

- **MBUX infotainment and display failures:** freezing, rebooting, black screens, lost navigation
- **Air suspension (Airmatic) problems:** sagging, warning messages, compressor failures
- **Electrical faults** and repeated warning lights
- **EV issues in the EQ lineup:** charging failures, range loss, and high-voltage warnings
- **Engine and transmission concerns,** including rough shifting and hesitation
- **Driver assistance malfunctions**

## Business-Owned Vehicles

California's lemon law also protects small businesses. A vehicle bought or used primarily for business can be covered if the business has five or fewer vehicles registered in California and the vehicle is under the weight limit. This matters for many Mercedes-Benz and Sprinter owners.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are Mercedes-Benz Sprinter vans covered by lemon law?
Sprinter vans used for personal purposes are covered. Vans owned by businesses may be covered if the business has five or fewer vehicles registered in California and the vehicle meets weight limits. An attorney can confirm coverage.

### Can I get a buyback on a leased Mercedes?
Yes. Leased vehicles with a new vehicle warranty are covered, and a lease buyback typically refunds your payments and pays off the lease.

### How long does a Mercedes lemon law case take?
Many cases resolve within a few months of the demand. Cases that go to litigation take longer. Recent California procedures require early document exchange and mediation in many cases, which can speed resolution.
