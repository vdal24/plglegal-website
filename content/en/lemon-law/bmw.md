## BMW Owners Expect More, and the Law Backs You Up

You paid a premium for a BMW. When your X3, X5, X7, 3 Series, 5 Series, 7 Series, M model, i4, i5, i7, or iX keeps going back to the service department for the same problem, California's lemon law can require BMW of North America to repurchase or replace the vehicle and pay your attorney fees.

## BMW Problems Owners Commonly Report

Examples of issues BMW owners commonly report:

- **Oil and coolant leaks,** including valve cover, oil filter housing, and water pump leaks
- **Electrical faults:** warning lights, battery drain, and module failures
- **iDrive and infotainment problems:** freezing screens, connectivity failures, camera faults
- **EV issues in the i4, i5, i7, and iX:** charging errors, range loss, and high-voltage system warnings
- **Transmission and drivetrain concerns**
- **Driver assistance and parking sensor malfunctions**

## Lease Buybacks Work Too

Many BMWs in California are leased. A lease buyback typically refunds the payments you made, including any down payment or drive-off fees, and pays off the remaining lease obligation, minus any usage offset.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does lemon law cover luxury vehicles like BMW?
Yes. California's lemon law covers new and leased vehicles of every price range. A higher purchase price usually means a larger buyback.

### Is my leased BMW covered?
Yes. Many BMW owners lease, and leased vehicles with a new vehicle warranty are covered. In a lease buyback, BMW typically refunds your payments and pays off the lease.

### Does BMW pay my attorney fees?
If you prevail, California law requires the manufacturer to pay your reasonable attorney fees and costs.
