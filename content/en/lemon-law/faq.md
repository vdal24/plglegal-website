## The Basics

### What is California's lemon law?
California's lemon law is part of the Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act. It requires manufacturers to repurchase or replace new vehicles that have warranty defects they cannot fix after a reasonable number of attempts.

### What vehicles are covered?
New cars, trucks, SUVs, and motorcycles sold or leased in California with a manufacturer's new vehicle warranty, demonstrator vehicles, and the chassis and drivetrain of motorhomes. Vehicles owned by small businesses with five or fewer vehicles registered in California can also qualify, subject to weight limits.

### Does it cover leased cars?
Yes.

### Does it cover used cars?
Only in limited situations after the California Supreme Court's 2024 *Rodriguez v. FCA US* decision. See [used car lemon law](/lemon-law/used-cars/).

### Does it matter where I bought the car?
The vehicle generally must have been sold or leased in California. If you bought it out of state, other state laws may apply.

## Repair Attempts

### How many repair attempts do I need?
California presumes a vehicle is a lemon if, within 18 months or 18,000 miles, a serious safety defect was repaired twice, the same defect was repaired four or more times, or the vehicle was out of service more than 30 days. Vehicles can qualify outside those numbers too.

### Do "could not duplicate" visits count?
They document that you reported the problem. Keep them.

### Does the problem have to be the same each time?
Not for the 30-day out-of-service presumption. For the repair-count presumptions, it is the same defect.

### What if the dealer refuses to write my complaint on the repair order?
Ask them to correct it before you leave. If they will not, write down what happened and send a short email to the dealer confirming your complaint.

## Money and Fees

### How much does a lemon law attorney cost?
In most cases, nothing out of pocket. The manufacturer must pay your attorney fees and costs if you prevail.

### What will I get back?
In a buyback, typically your down payment, monthly payments, loan or lease payoff, taxes, fees, and finance charges, minus a mileage offset. See [buyback calculation](/lemon-law/buyback-calculation/).

### Can I get more than a refund?
If the manufacturer willfully violated the law, a court can award a civil penalty of up to two times your actual damages.

### Will I have to keep making payments?
Yes. Keep your loan or lease current and keep insurance on the car while the case is pending. Missing payments can hurt your credit and your case.

## Timing and Process

### How long do I have?
It depends on whether your manufacturer opted into California's 2025 lemon law procedures. For those manufacturers, a lawsuit generally must be filed within one year after the warranty expires and within six years of delivery. Do not wait.

### What changed in 2025?
Manufacturers could opt into new procedures that require a written demand at least 30 days before filing suit, give the manufacturer 30 days to offer a buyback or replacement, and add early document exchange and mediation once a case is filed.

### How long does a case take?
Many cases resolve within a few months after the demand. Litigated cases take longer.

### Do I have to go to court?
Most cases settle. If yours goes to trial, we prepare you every step of the way.

### Do I have to use the manufacturer's arbitration program?
Generally no. Some manufacturers offer certified arbitration programs, but using them is usually optional. Talk to us before you agree to arbitrate.

## Practical Questions

### Can I sell or trade in the car?
Talk to a lawyer first. Selling or trading in the car can complicate or end your claim.

### Can I keep driving it?
Yes, if it is safe. If it is not safe to drive, have it towed and keep the receipts.
