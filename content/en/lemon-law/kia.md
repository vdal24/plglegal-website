## Kia Owners

Kia's lineup has grown quickly, from the Telluride, Sorento, Sportage, and Carnival to the K5, Forte, Soul, Seltos, and the EV6 and EV9. When one of these vehicles keeps going back to the dealer for the same defect, California's lemon law may require Kia to repurchase or replace it, or pay you to keep it, with Kia paying your attorney fees if you prevail.

## Kia Problems Owners Commonly Report

Examples of reported issues and recalls:

- **Engine failures** in certain models with Theta II and related engines, which led to recalls and extended warranty programs
- **ABS module fire risk,** which led Kia to recall certain vehicles and advise some owners to park outside
- **EV6 and EV9 charging problems,** including the integrated charging control unit (ICCU), which has been subject to recall
- **Transmission and dual-clutch concerns**
- **Electrical and infotainment failures,** camera faults, and battery drain
- **Engine oil consumption and stalling**

## Your Rights Beyond the Recall

A recall notice or extended warranty does not replace your lemon law rights. If your Kia keeps having the same problem, the law may entitle you to more than another repair. Do not sign a release without talking to a lawyer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does a Kia engine recall mean I have a lemon law case?
Not by itself. If the recall repair fails, the engine problem returns, or your Kia spent a long time out of service, you may have a claim. Keep every repair and recall record.

### Can I file a lemon law claim on a leased Kia?
Yes. Leased vehicles with a manufacturer's new vehicle warranty are covered under California's lemon law.

### What does it cost to hire you?
Nothing out of pocket in most cases. If you prevail, California law requires the manufacturer to pay your attorney fees and costs.
