*Written by Vardan Daldumyan, Esq., former in-house trial attorney for an insurance company.*

When I worked for an insurance company, I saw the same mistakes reduce claim values over and over. Most happened in the first few days after the crash. Here is what I tell clients to do.

## At the Scene

### 1. Check for injuries and call 911
Call 911 if anyone is hurt. A police report creates an official record of the crash and the parties involved.

### 2. Move to safety
If the cars can be moved and no one is hurt, move out of traffic. On a freeway, stay in the vehicle with your seatbelt on if you cannot safely exit.

### 3. Photograph everything
Take photos and video of both vehicles, license plates, the scene, skid marks, traffic signals, road conditions, and your injuries. Photos taken at the scene are some of the strongest evidence in a case.

### 4. Exchange information
Get the other driver's name, phone number, driver's license number, license plate, and insurance company and policy number. Photograph their license and insurance card.

### 5. Find witnesses
Get names and phone numbers of anyone who saw the crash. Witnesses leave quickly, and they are hard to find later.

### 6. Do not apologize or argue fault
Saying "I'm sorry" can be used as an admission. Stick to the facts.

## In the Days After

### 7. Get medical care, even if you feel okay
Adrenaline hides injuries. Neck, back, and head injuries often show up hours or days later. Gaps in treatment are one of the first things adjusters use to argue you were not really hurt.

### 8. Report the crash to the DMV if required
In California, you must report a crash to the DMV within 10 days if anyone was injured or killed, or property damage exceeded $1,000. This is form SR-1, and it is separate from any police report.

### 9. Notify your own insurance company
Your policy requires prompt notice. Give basic facts only.

### 10. Do not give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurer
Their adjuster will sound friendly. The recorded statement exists to lock you into details and find inconsistencies. You are not required to give one. Talk to a lawyer first.

### 11. Stay off social media
Insurance companies check. A photo of you at a family event can be twisted into evidence that you are not hurt.

### 12. Keep records
Save medical bills, receipts, mileage to appointments, and notes on how your injuries affect your daily life and work.

## Mistakes That Cost Injured People Money

- Accepting the first offer, which is rarely the real number
- Signing a medical authorization that gives the insurer access to your entire medical history
- Waiting too long to see a doctor
- Missing the deadline: generally two years for an injury lawsuit, and only six months for a claim against a government agency
