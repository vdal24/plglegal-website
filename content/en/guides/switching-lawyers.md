## You Are Allowed to Change Lawyers

Hiring a lawyer is not a permanent decision. In California, **the client can end the attorney-client relationship at any time.** That is true before a lawsuit, after one is filed, and even if you signed a contingency fee agreement.

Many people stay with a lawyer they have lost confidence in because they think leaving will cost them money or damage the case. In most situations, neither is true.

## Common Reasons Clients Switch

- **You cannot reach your lawyer.** Calls and messages go unanswered for weeks.
- **You only ever talk to a case manager.** You have never had a real conversation with the attorney.
- **Nothing is happening.** No demand has been sent, or the case has not moved in months.
- **You are being pushed to settle** for a number nobody will explain.
- **The lawyer says fault is a problem** and seems ready to give up.
- **You no longer trust the advice you are getting.**

## What It Costs

In a contingency fee case you generally do **not** pay two full fees. The lawyer you leave is usually entitled to the reasonable value of the work already performed, and that amount typically comes out of the one contingency fee at the end of the case. The lawyers divide a single fee. In most cases you do not pay more because you changed counsel. Every fee agreement is different, and we will review yours with you before you decide.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I fire my personal injury lawyer in California?
Yes. A client has the right to end the relationship with a lawyer at any time, with or without a reason, including in a contingency fee case.

### Will I have to pay two attorney fees?
Usually not. In a contingency case, the first lawyer is generally entitled to the reasonable value of the work already done, which is typically paid out of the single contingency fee at the end of the case and divided between the lawyers. Review your fee agreement, and ask us to walk you through it.

### Do I have to tell my current lawyer myself?
No. Once you sign with a new attorney, the new office sends the notice and requests your file. Your former lawyer is required to release the file to you or your new counsel.

### Is it too late to switch if a lawsuit is already filed?
No. Attorneys are substituted in pending lawsuits regularly. It is easier earlier in the case, and it becomes harder close to trial, so do not wait if you have concerns.

### Will switching delay my case?
There is a short transition while the file is transferred. A case that was stalled can pick up again once someone is actively working it. We review the file and give you a plan before you decide.
