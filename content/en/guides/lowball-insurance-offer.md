## Why First Offers Come In Low

Attorney Vardan Daldumyan worked as an in-house trial attorney for an insurance company before founding Prospect Law Group. From that side of the table, low first offers are not personal and not an accident. They are how the system works:

- **The adjuster has a range, not a number.** Offers usually start near the bottom of the adjuster's settlement authority.
- **Early offers come before the full picture.** An offer made while you are still treating cannot account for care you have not had yet.
- **Unrepresented claims are valued differently.** The insurer knows a person without a lawyer is unlikely to file a lawsuit.
- **Software and guidelines discount what is not documented.** Gaps in treatment, vague medical records, and prior injuries all lower the evaluation.
- **Fault is negotiable.** Assigning you a share of the blame is one of the easiest ways to cut an offer.

For more on how claims are evaluated, see [how insurance adjusters evaluate your injury claim](/guides/insurance-adjuster-tactics/).

## Signs an Offer Is Too Low

- It does not cover your medical bills to date
- It ignores future treatment your doctor has recommended
- It includes nothing, or very little, for lost income
- It arrived within days or weeks of the collision
- It came with pressure: "this is our final offer" or "this offer expires Friday"
- The adjuster blames you for part of the crash without explaining why
- The adjuster will not put the reasons for the number in writing

## What to Do Before You Respond {: .sec-steps }

1. **Do not sign anything yet.** A release ends the claim for good. Do not cash a check marked as final payment either.
2. **Get the offer in writing.** Ask the adjuster to explain in writing how the number was reached and what was discounted.
3. **Add up the real losses.** Medical bills, future care, lost income, and how the injury has affected your daily life. See [what is my case worth](/guides/car-accident-case-value-california/).
4. **Get a free review.** Send us the offer. We will tell you whether it is in a fair range and what it would take to improve it.

## How a Lawyer Changes the Evaluation

An insurance company evaluates risk. A well-documented claim, presented by an attorney who is prepared to file suit, carries more risk for the insurer than the same claim presented by someone who cannot. Our work is to:

- Gather the medical records and bills and make sure they say what they need to say
- Document lost income and future care
- Answer the fault arguments with evidence
- Identify every insurance policy that applies, including your own [underinsured motorist coverage](/practice-areas/uninsured-motorist-claims/)
- Send a demand that gives the insurer a reason and a deadline to pay
- File a lawsuit if the insurer will not be reasonable

No lawyer can guarantee a higher offer. We will give you an honest opinion about whether we can help, at no charge.

## If It Is Your Own Insurance Company

Uninsured motorist, underinsured motorist, and medical payments claims are made against your own insurer, which owes you a duty of good faith. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I accept the insurance company's first offer?
Not until you know what the claim is worth. A first offer is often made before the adjuster has your full medical picture. Once you sign a release, you generally cannot ask for more, even if you need further treatment.

### Can I negotiate with the adjuster myself?
Yes, and for small claims with minor injuries that can be reasonable. For serious injuries, disputed fault, or an offer that does not cover your bills, a free review with an attorney costs you nothing and tells you where you stand.

### I already have a lawyer and the offers are still low. What can I do?
Ask your lawyer to explain how the offer compares with your damages and what the plan is. If you are not getting answers, you have the right to a second opinion and the right to [change lawyers](/guides/switching-lawyers/).

### Is there a deadline to respond to an offer?
Adjusters sometimes put a short deadline on an offer to create pressure. The deadline that matters most is the statute of limitations, which for most California injury claims is two years from the accident, and six months to present a claim against a government agency.
