*Written by Vardan Daldumyan, Esq., former in-house trial attorney for an insurance company.*

I used to work on the other side of the table. Here is what happens to your claim after you report it, and what you can do about it.

## The Reserve

Soon after a claim is opened, the insurer sets a **reserve**: an estimate of what the claim will cost. Reserves are adjusted as information comes in. Early on, they are based on limited facts, like the police report, the property damage, and what you have said so far.

What you do in the first weeks shapes that number.

## What Adjusters Look For to Reduce Value

- **Gaps in treatment.** If you waited two weeks to see a doctor, the adjuster will argue the crash did not cause your injury.
- **Prior injuries.** They will request records to argue your pain is from an old condition.
- **Low property damage.** Insurers often argue that minor vehicle damage means minor injuries, even though medicine does not support that rule.
- **Inconsistent statements.** Differences between your recorded statement, the police report, and your medical records.
- **Social media.** Photos or posts suggesting you are active or unhurt.
- **Unrepresented claimants.** People without lawyers are less likely to file suit, which lowers the risk the insurer is pricing in.

## How Offers Are Built

Adjusters typically use claim evaluation tools and internal guidelines. Medical treatment, diagnosis codes, the type of providers you saw, and the duration of care all feed the evaluation. Then the adjuster has a settlement authority range. The first offer is usually near the bottom of it.

## How to Protect Your Claim

1. **Get treatment promptly** and follow your doctor's plan
2. **Tell every provider about every symptom,** consistently
3. **Do not give a recorded statement** to the other insurer without advice
4. **Do not sign a broad medical authorization**
5. **Keep a pain and activity journal**
6. **Talk to a lawyer before accepting any offer**

## Why This Matters

The insurance company is evaluating risk. A claim that is well documented, prepared for trial, and handled by a lawyer who knows how the evaluation works carries more risk for the insurer. That is what moves the number.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is the first settlement offer so low?
First offers are typically made before the adjuster has full information or before you have a lawyer. They test whether you will accept less. The adjuster's settlement authority is usually higher.

### Is the adjuster on my side?
No. Even a friendly adjuster works for the insurance company, and their job is to resolve the claim for as little as reasonably possible.

### Does hiring a lawyer change how the insurance company treats my claim?
Often, yes. Represented claims are evaluated with the risk of litigation in mind, which changes how insurers value them.
