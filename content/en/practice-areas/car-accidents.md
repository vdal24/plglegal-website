## California Rules That Affect Your Claim

- **Deadline:** two years for injury lawsuits, three years for property damage, six months for claims against a government agency
- **Comparative fault:** you can recover even if partly at fault, reduced by your share
- **Proposition 213:** uninsured drivers generally cannot recover pain and suffering damages from an insured at-fault driver, with some exceptions
- **Minimum insurance:** California's minimum liability limits increased in 2025 to $30,000 per person, $60,000 per accident, and $15,000 for property damage. Many drivers carry only the minimum, which is why your own uninsured and underinsured coverage matters.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long do I have to file a car accident claim in California?
Generally two years from the accident for injury claims and three years for property damage. If a government vehicle or road condition was involved, a written claim must be filed within six months.

### What if I was partly at fault?
California uses pure comparative fault. You can still recover, reduced by your percentage of fault. If you were 20% at fault, you can recover 80% of your damages.

### Should I give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurance company?
Not before talking to a lawyer. Recorded statements are used to find inconsistencies and reduce the value of your claim.

### What if the other driver has no insurance?
Your own uninsured motorist coverage may pay for your injuries. We help you make that claim and deal with your insurer.
