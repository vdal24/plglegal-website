## Why It Matters That the Other Driver Was Working

A crash with a delivery van, a contractor's pickup, a company sedan, or a box truck looks like any other collision. Legally it is not. If the driver was on the job, the **employer is usually responsible** for what the employee did, and commercial auto policies generally carry higher limits than a personal policy.

That matters most when injuries are serious. It also means you are up against a commercial insurer with experienced adjusters and defense lawyers. Attorney Vardan Daldumyan worked as an in-house trial attorney for an insurance company. He knows how those claims are defended.

## Who May Be Responsible

- **The driver,** for careless driving
- **The employer,** for the acts of an employee on the job
- **The employer directly,** for negligent hiring, training, supervision, or scheduling that pushes drivers to rush
- **The vehicle owner or a leasing company**
- **A maintenance contractor,** when bad brakes, tires, or repairs played a part
- **A company that hired the contractor,** depending on the contracts and control involved

Companies often argue the driver was "off the clock," on a personal errand, or an independent contractor. Those defenses turn on evidence that the company controls, which is why it has to be requested and preserved early.

## Evidence We Move to Preserve

- Driver logs, dispatch records, delivery app data, and GPS or telematics
- Dash camera and nearby surveillance video
- The driver's employment file, training records, and driving history
- Vehicle inspection and maintenance records
- Cell phone records, when distraction is suspected
- The company's insurance policies, including umbrella coverage

Much of this is kept for a limited time. A preservation letter sent early can keep it from being lost.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I bring a claim against the company if its employee caused the crash?
Often, yes. In California an employer is generally responsible for harm an employee causes while doing the job. A company can also be directly responsible for careless hiring, training, supervision, or vehicle maintenance.

### What if the driver was an independent contractor?
Many delivery and gig drivers are labeled contractors. The label does not always decide the question. Responsibility depends on the real working relationship, the contracts, and the insurance in place. We investigate all of it.

### What if a city, county, or government vehicle hit me?
You can bring a claim, but the deadline is short. A written claim generally must be presented to the public agency within six months of the collision.

### The company's insurer called me right away. Should I talk to them?
Be careful. Commercial insurers move fast because early statements and quick settlements save them money. You are not required to give a recorded statement to the other side's insurance company. Talk to a lawyer first. See [how adjusters evaluate claims](/guides/insurance-adjuster-tactics/).

### What if I was driving for work when I was hit?
You may have a workers' compensation claim through your employer and a separate injury claim against the driver who caused the crash. The two interact, so get advice early.
