## Is Your Car a Lemon?

If your new or leased vehicle keeps going back to the dealer for the same problem and the dealer cannot fix it, California's Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act may require the manufacturer to:

- **Buy back your vehicle** (a repurchase), refunding what you paid including your down payment, monthly payments, taxes, registration, and finance charges, minus a usage offset
- **Replace your vehicle** with a comparable new one
- **Pay you cash** to keep the car (a "cash and keep" settlement)
- **Pay your attorney fees and costs**, which the law requires when the consumer prevails

California has one of the strongest lemon laws in the country. Manufacturers know it, and they still deny valid claims every day, hoping owners give up.

## Does My Vehicle Qualify?

California's lemon law protects buyers and lessees of new vehicles, including cars, trucks, SUVs, motorcycles, and the vehicle portion of motorhomes, sold or leased in California with a manufacturer's new vehicle warranty. Demonstrator vehicles sold with a new car warranty generally qualify too.

Your vehicle may be a lemon if:

1. **It has a defect covered by the warranty** that substantially impairs its use, value, or safety
2. **The manufacturer or its dealer had a reasonable number of chances** to fix it
3. **The problem was not caused by abuse** or unauthorized modifications

### The Lemon Law Presumption (Tanner Act)

California presumes a vehicle is a lemon if, within the first **18 months or 18,000 miles**, whichever comes first:

- A defect likely to cause **death or serious injury** was subject to repair **two or more times**; or
- The **same defect** was subject to repair **four or more times**; or
- The vehicle was **out of service for repairs more than 30 days** total.

For the first two, if your warranty booklet or owner's manual clearly requires it, you must also notify the manufacturer directly, not just the dealer.

The presumption is just one path. Many vehicles qualify outside 18 months or 18,000 miles, as long as the defect first showed up during the warranty period.

## Common Lemon Law Defects

These are examples of problems that frequently lead to lemon law claims when the dealer cannot fix them:

- **Transmission problems:** harsh or delayed shifting, slipping, jerking, hesitation, or failure. [Transmission lemon law](/lemon-law/defects/transmission/)
- **Engine problems:** stalling, loss of power, oil consumption, overheating, check engine lights that keep returning. [Engine defects](/lemon-law/defects/engine/)
- **Electrical and infotainment failures:** dead screens, cameras that black out, warning lights, battery drain. [Electrical defects](/lemon-law/defects/electrical-infotainment/)
- **EV and hybrid battery problems:** range loss, charging failures, reduced power warnings. [EV battery lemon law](/lemon-law/defects/ev-battery/)
- **Safety systems:** brakes, steering, airbags, automatic emergency braking activating without reason
- **Leaks:** water leaks into the cabin, coolant or oil leaks
- **Suspension and steering:** vibrations, pulling, clunking

## How a Lemon Law Case Works

1. **Free case review.** Send us your purchase or lease contract and your repair orders. We tell you honestly whether you have a case.
2. **Demand to the manufacturer.** We send a written demand for a buyback or replacement. For manufacturers that opted into California's 2025 procedures, this notice must be sent at least 30 days before filing suit, and the manufacturer has 30 days to make an offer.
3. **Negotiation.** Many cases resolve at this stage.
4. **Lawsuit, if needed.** If the manufacturer refuses a fair resolution, we file suit. Recent procedural changes include early document exchange and mandatory mediation in many cases.
5. **Resolution.** You receive your buyback, replacement, or cash settlement, and the manufacturer pays our fees.

## What You Can Recover

In a repurchase, the manufacturer typically must refund:

- Your down payment and all monthly payments made
- The remaining loan or lease balance (paid off directly)
- Sales tax, registration, license fees, and other official fees
- Finance charges and certain incidental costs such as towing and rental cars

The manufacturer may deduct a **usage offset** based on the miles you drove before first taking the car in for the defect. If the manufacturer willfully violated the law, a court can award a **civil penalty of up to two times your actual damages**.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I have to pay a lawyer for a lemon law case?
Usually not. California's lemon law requires the manufacturer to pay the consumer's attorney fees and costs when the consumer prevails, so most clients pay nothing out of pocket.

### How many repair attempts make a car a lemon in California?
There is no single magic number. California presumes a vehicle is a lemon if, within 18 months or 18,000 miles, the same serious safety defect was repaired twice, the same defect was repaired four or more times, or the car was out of service for more than 30 days. A vehicle can still qualify outside those numbers.

### Does California lemon law cover leased cars?
Yes. Leased vehicles sold or leased in California with a manufacturer's new vehicle warranty are covered.

### Does lemon law cover used cars?
Sometimes. After the California Supreme Court's 2024 decision in *Rodriguez v. FCA US*, a used car that only carries the remainder of the original factory warranty generally does not qualify as a "new motor vehicle." Other warranty claims may still apply, so have an attorney review your paperwork.

### How long do I have to file a lemon law claim?
It depends on your manufacturer. For manufacturers that opted into California's 2025 lemon law procedures, a lawsuit generally must be filed within one year after the warranty expires and within six years of delivery. Other manufacturers follow the older rules. Call us as early as possible.
