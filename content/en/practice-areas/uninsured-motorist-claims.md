## The Other Driver Has No Insurance. Now What?

Many drivers on Los Angeles roads carry no insurance, and many more carry only the state minimum, which since 2025 is $30,000 per person and $60,000 per accident. A serious injury can exceed that in a single hospital visit.

That is what **uninsured motorist (UM)** and **underinsured motorist (UIM)** coverage on your own policy is for. It steps into the shoes of the driver who should have paid.

There is a catch. In a UM or UIM claim, **your own insurance company becomes your opponent.** It evaluates your claim the same way it would evaluate a stranger's, with the same goal of paying less. ## When UM and UIM Coverage Applies

- **The at-fault driver has no insurance**
- **Hit-and-run,** when the driver cannot be identified (special rules apply, see below)
- **The at-fault driver's policy is too small** for your injuries, and your own UIM limit is higher
- **You were a passenger, pedestrian, or cyclist** hit by an uninsured driver
- **Rideshare trips:** California reduced the UM/UIM coverage Uber and Lyft must carry starting in 2026, which makes your own policy more important. See [rideshare accidents](/practice-areas/rideshare-accidents/).

## Rules That Trip People Up

- **Hit-and-run:** California generally requires physical contact with the other vehicle, a police report within 24 hours, and a statement to your insurer within 30 days.
- **Underinsured claims:** you generally must first collect the at-fault driver's full policy limits. Your UIM coverage then pays the difference up to your own limit, reduced by what the other policy paid.
- **Deadlines:** UM claims have their own time limits, which can be as short as two years from the collision to settle, file suit, or formally demand arbitration.
- **Arbitration, not a jury:** if you and your insurer cannot agree, most UM/UIM disputes are decided by an arbitrator.
- **Recorded statements and medical authorizations:** your policy requires cooperation, but that does not mean handing over your entire medical history without advice.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage?
Uninsured motorist (UM) coverage applies when the at-fault driver has no liability insurance or cannot be identified, as in many hit-and-run crashes. Underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage applies when the at-fault driver has insurance, but less than your own UM/UIM limit and not enough to cover your injuries.

### Will my rates go up if I make an uninsured motorist claim?
California law generally prohibits an insurer from raising your premium because of an accident in which you were not principally at fault.

### Do I have this coverage?
Insurers in California must offer uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage with every auto policy, and it can only be declined in writing. Check the declarations page of your policy, or send it to us and we will review it for free.

### What if it was a hit-and-run?
Uninsured motorist coverage can apply to a hit-and-run, but California generally requires physical contact with the other vehicle, a report to the police within 24 hours, and a statement to your insurer within 30 days. Act quickly.

### Can I settle with the at-fault driver first?
Be careful. In an underinsured motorist claim you generally must collect the at-fault driver's policy limits first, and settling the wrong way or without notice to your own insurer can put your coverage at risk. Get advice before you sign.

### Does this apply if I was a passenger, a pedestrian, or on a bicycle?
It can. Your own auto policy's UM/UIM coverage often follows you, and a passenger may also be covered by the policy on the car they were riding in. Coverage depends on the policy language.
