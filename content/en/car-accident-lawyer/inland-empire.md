## Where Inland Empire Crashes Happen

- **The 10, 15, and 215 freeways,** with heavy commuter and big rig traffic, high speeds, and sudden slowdowns
- **The 60 and 91 freeways,** with rush-hour rear-end and sideswipe crashes
- **The Cajon Pass and the high desert,** with steep grades, wind, and truck traffic
- **Busy surface streets** like Foothill Boulevard, Base Line, Magnolia Avenue, and University Avenue, with left-turn and intersection collisions

## What to Do After a Crash

1. Call 911 and get medical help.
2. Photograph the vehicles, the scene, and your injuries.
3. Get the other driver's insurance and license information.
4. See a doctor promptly, even if you feel okay.
5. Do not give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurer.
6. Call us.

Full checklist: [What to do after a car accident in California](/guides/what-to-do-after-car-accident-california/)

## Important Deadlines

- **2 years** for most personal injury lawsuits in California
- **3 years** for property damage
- **6 months** to file a government claim if a public agency, city vehicle, or bus was involved

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I have to drive to Glendale to meet you?
No. We handle Inland Empire cases by phone, text, and video, and we can come to you if you are injured. Most clients never need to visit our office.

### Who takes the police report for a crash in the Inland Empire?
Crashes on freeways like the 10, 15, 215, 60, and 91 are generally handled by the California Highway Patrol. Crashes on city streets are handled by the local police department or the county sheriff, depending on the city.

### Was a city vehicle, bus, or public road involved?
Claims against a public agency generally require a written government claim within six months. Call us right away so that deadline is not missed.
