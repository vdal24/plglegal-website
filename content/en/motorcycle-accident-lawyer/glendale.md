## Riding Around Glendale

Glendale is a gateway for riders. The Angeles Crest Highway (Route 2) climbs into the San Gabriel Mountains just north of La Cañada Flintridge, and riders from across Southern California pass through Glendale to reach it. In town, riders deal with crowded freeways, sudden lane changes on the 134, and drivers who simply do not look.

## The Bias Riders Face

Insurance adjusters often assume the motorcyclist was speeding or reckless. That assumption drives low offers. We counter it with evidence:

- Scene photos, video, and nearby surveillance footage
- Witness statements
- Vehicle damage and crash reconstruction
- Your riding history and safety gear

## Common Motorcycle Crash Causes

- Left-turning cars that fail to yield
- Drivers changing lanes without checking blind spots
- Rear-end collisions in stop-and-go traffic
- Road hazards: potholes, gravel, debris on mountain roads
- Opened car doors in downtown traffic

Lane splitting is legal in California, and a rider who was lawfully lane splitting is not automatically at fault.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is lane splitting legal in California?
Yes. California law recognizes lane splitting. Insurers sometimes still argue that a rider who was lane splitting was at fault, but legal lane splitting does not by itself make the rider responsible.

### What if I was not wearing a helmet?
California requires helmets. Not wearing one can be used to argue reduced damages for head injuries, but it does not bar your claim, especially for injuries unrelated to your head.
