How Much Is My Car Accident Case Worth in California?

What determines the value of a California car accident claim: medical costs, lost wages, pain and suffering, insurance limits, and fault. Explained by a former insurance company attorney.

Call 323-452-5222Free Case Review

Written by Vardan Daldumyan, Esq., former in-house trial attorney for an insurance company.

There is no honest way to put a number on a case without reviewing the facts. But the factors insurers use are predictable. Here is what drives value.

1. Your Medical Damages

Medical treatment is the foundation of most injury claims: emergency care, imaging, physical therapy, injections, surgery, and future care. Under California law, recoverable past medical expenses are generally limited to the amount actually paid or owed, not the full billed amount. Future care is estimated by your doctors.

The type of injury matters most. Fractures, herniated discs needing injections or surgery, traumatic brain injuries, and permanent impairments are valued far higher than short-term sprains.

2. Lost Income

Wages you missed while recovering, plus any reduction in your ability to earn in the future. Self-employed people and gig workers can recover too, with the right documentation.

3. Pain, Suffering, and Life Impact

Non-economic damages compensate for physical pain, emotional distress, sleep loss, anxiety about driving, and the activities you can no longer do. There is no formula in California law. These damages depend on how well your story is documented and how credible it is.

4. Fault

California uses pure comparative fault. If you are found 25% at fault, your recovery is reduced by 25%. Disputed liability is one of the biggest value swings in any case, which is why early evidence like photos, witnesses, and video matters.

5. Available Insurance

Practically, a case is often limited by the insurance available:

  • The at-fault driver's liability limits. California's minimum is $30,000 per person as of 2025, and many drivers carry only that.
  • Your own underinsured motorist coverage
  • Other responsible parties, such as an employer, rideshare company, or commercial vehicle owner
  • Rideshare policies, which changed in 2026

6. Factors That Reduce Value

  • Gaps in treatment
  • Pre-existing conditions (though aggravation of a prior condition is compensable)
  • Inconsistent statements
  • Being uninsured yourself, which under Proposition 213 generally bars pain and suffering damages against an insured driver

Why the Insurer's Number and the Real Number Differ

Insurers evaluate risk. The more prepared your case is for trial, the more risk it carries for them, and the closer the offer gets to what the case is truly worth.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there an average settlement for a car accident in California?

Averages are misleading because every case depends on injuries, treatment, fault, and available insurance. A soft-tissue case and a surgery case can differ by many times. A free case review gives a far better estimate.

Can I recover more than the other driver's insurance limits?

Sometimes. Options include your own underinsured motorist coverage, other responsible parties, and in some situations the at-fault driver's personal assets or an insurer's liability for unreasonably refusing to settle within limits.

Do I pay taxes on a personal injury settlement?

Compensation for physical injuries is generally not taxable under federal law, but some parts, like interest or punitive damages, may be. Ask a tax professional about your situation.
