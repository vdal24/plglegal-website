## The Rules Changed in 2024

For years, many California used car buyers could bring lemon law claims if the car was still under the original manufacturer's warranty. In October 2024, the California Supreme Court decided *Rodriguez v. FCA US*, holding that a used vehicle sold with only the balance of the original manufacturer's warranty is generally not a "new motor vehicle" under the lemon law's repurchase provisions.

That narrowed the path for many used car buyers. It did not eliminate every option.

## What May Still Protect You

- **A dealer's own written warranty.** If the dealer gave you a warranty, it may carry obligations under California's Song-Beverly Act.
- **Certified pre-owned (CPO) warranties.** A manufacturer CPO program often includes a separate written warranty that can create rights.
- **Demonstrator vehicles.** Demos sold with a new car warranty are generally covered as new vehicles.
- **Implied warranty of merchantability.** Used goods sold with an express warranty can carry implied warranty protections for a limited period.
- **Other consumer laws.** Misrepresentation, undisclosed damage, or a salvage history that was hidden may support other claims.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I file a lemon law claim on a used car I bought from a dealer?
Possibly, but the rules changed in 2024. A used car that only carries the remainder of the original factory warranty generally no longer qualifies as a "new motor vehicle." A dealer-issued warranty, a certified pre-owned warranty, or other warranty claims may still give you rights.

### What about certified pre-owned vehicles?
Certified pre-owned vehicles often come with a separate written warranty. That warranty may create its own rights under California's warranty laws. An attorney should review the CPO paperwork.

### Does the used car rule apply to demonstrator vehicles?
Demonstrator vehicles sold with a manufacturer's new car warranty are treated differently from used cars and are generally still covered.
