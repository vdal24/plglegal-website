## Honda and Acura Owners

Honda and Acura vehicles are among the most common on California roads. When a CR-V, Civic, Accord, HR-V, Pilot, Passport, Odyssey, Ridgeline, Prologue, or Acura MDX, RDX, Integra, or TLX keeps returning to the dealer for the same issue, California's lemon law may entitle you to a buyback, replacement, or cash settlement, with your attorney fees paid by the manufacturer.

## Honda and Acura Problems Owners Commonly Report

Examples of reported issues, some of which have been the subject of federal investigations:

- **Unexpected automatic emergency braking,** which was the subject of a federal safety investigation into certain Accord and CR-V models
- **Oil dilution in 1.5L turbocharged engines,** reported by owners of some earlier CR-V and Civic models
- **Infotainment and display failures:** freezing, rebooting, Bluetooth and CarPlay dropouts
- **Transmission and CVT concerns:** hesitation, judder, rough shifting
- **Air conditioning failures**
- **Hybrid system warnings** in Accord, CR-V, and Civic hybrids

## What Makes a Strong Honda Lemon Law Claim

- The same complaint appears on multiple repair orders
- The problem affects safety, drivability, or value
- The vehicle has spent significant time at the dealer
- The problem first appeared while the warranty was in effect

Even if the dealer wrote "could not duplicate," those visits still count as documentation that you reported the problem.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can sudden automatic braking be a lemon law defect?
Yes. A driver assistance system that brakes without reason can substantially impair safety. If the dealer cannot fix it after a reasonable number of attempts, it may qualify under California's lemon law.

### Is Acura covered by California lemon law?
Yes. Acura vehicles sold or leased in California with a new vehicle warranty are covered.

### The dealer keeps updating software but the problem returns. What should I do?
Keep every repair order showing the update and your complaint. Repeated software updates for the same problem can count as repair attempts. Have an attorney review your records.
