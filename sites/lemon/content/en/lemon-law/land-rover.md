## Land Rover and Range Rover Owners

Range Rover, Range Rover Sport, Range Rover Velar, Range Rover Evoque, Defender, and Discovery owners expect luxury and capability. Too often they get a loaner car and a long wait. If your vehicle keeps going back for warranty repairs, California's lemon law may require Jaguar Land Rover North America to repurchase or replace it and pay your attorney fees.

## Problems Owners Commonly Report

- **Electrical faults:** warning lights, battery drain, keyless entry and module failures
- **Infotainment problems:** freezing or rebooting screens, lost connectivity, camera failures
- **Air suspension failures:** sagging, error messages, compressor problems
- **Engine and cooling system issues:** coolant leaks and overheating warnings
- **Plug-in hybrid (PHEV) charging problems**
- **Water leaks** and sunroof drainage issues

## Days Out of Service Add Up

Land Rover owners often have a vehicle with several different problems rather than one. Under California law, a vehicle that has been out of service for warranty repairs more than 30 days total within 18 months or 18,000 miles is presumed to be a lemon, even if each visit was for a different issue. Keep every repair order and note your loaner dates.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### My Range Rover has had many different problems, not the same one. Can it still be a lemon?
Possibly. Repeated repairs of the same defect are the most common path, but a vehicle that spent more than 30 days out of service for warranty repairs in its first 18 months or 18,000 miles is presumed a lemon even if the problems differ.

### Is Jaguar covered too?
Yes. Jaguar and Land Rover vehicles sold or leased in California with a new vehicle warranty are both covered.

### Can a business-owned Range Rover qualify?
It can, if the business has five or fewer vehicles registered in California and the vehicle meets the weight limits.
