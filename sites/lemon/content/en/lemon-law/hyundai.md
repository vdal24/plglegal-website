## Hyundai and Genesis Owners

Hyundai backs its vehicles with one of the longest powertrain warranties in the industry. A long warranty is only useful if the dealer can actually fix the problem. When your Tucson, Santa Fe, Palisade, Elantra, Sonata, Kona, Ioniq 5, Ioniq 6, or Genesis keeps failing, California's lemon law can require Hyundai to buy it back or replace it instead of sending you back for another repair.

## Hyundai Problems Owners Commonly Report

Examples of reported issues and recalls:

- **Engine failures** in certain models with Theta II and related engines, which led to multiple recalls and extended warranty programs
- **ABS module fire risk,** which led Hyundai to recall certain vehicles and advise some owners to park outside
- **EV charging failures** in Ioniq models, including the integrated charging control unit (ICCU), which has been subject to recall
- **Transmission problems:** dual-clutch hesitation, jerking, and shifting faults
- **Electrical and infotainment failures**
- **Oil consumption and engine knocking**

## Recalls and Extended Warranties Are Not the End of Your Rights

Hyundai may tell you a recall or extended warranty takes care of the problem. If the defect keeps returning or your vehicle spends weeks at the dealer, you may still have a lemon law claim. Do not sign a release or accept a "goodwill" offer without advice.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Hyundai has a long warranty. Why would I need lemon law?
A long warranty only promises repairs. If Hyundai cannot fix the defect after a reasonable number of attempts, California's lemon law can require a buyback or replacement instead of more trips to the dealer.

### My Ioniq stopped charging. Is that a lemon law issue?
It can be. Charging system failures that leave the car undrivable or unable to charge substantially impair its use. If the problem recurs after repairs, have it reviewed.

### Is Genesis covered?
Yes. Genesis vehicles sold or leased in California with a new vehicle warranty are covered.
