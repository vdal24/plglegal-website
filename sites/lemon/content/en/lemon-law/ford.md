## Ford Owners Have Strong Rights in California

Ford builds some of the best-selling trucks and SUVs in California, and it also issues a large number of recalls and service bulletins. If your F-150, Super Duty, Ranger, Explorer, Expedition, Escape, Bronco, Bronco Sport, Maverick, Mustang, Mustang Mach-E, or F-150 Lightning keeps going back to the dealer for the same problem, you may be entitled to a buyback.

California's Song-Beverly Consumer Warranty Act does not care how popular the vehicle is. If Ford or its dealers cannot fix a warranty defect within a reasonable number of attempts, Ford may have to:

- Repurchase the vehicle and refund your payments, down payment, taxes, and fees
- Replace it with a comparable new Ford
- Pay you cash to keep it
- Pay your attorney fees

## Ford Problems Owners Commonly Report

These are examples of issues Ford owners have reported. Whether your vehicle qualifies depends on your repair history, not the model alone.

- **10-speed automatic transmission:** harsh, delayed, or erratic shifting in vehicles such as the F-150, Expedition, Ranger, and Bronco
- **Engine concerns:** oil leaks, coolant leaks, loss of power, and repeated check engine lights
- **Electrical and SYNC infotainment problems:** frozen screens, failed backup cameras, battery drain
- **EV issues in the Mustang Mach-E and F-150 Lightning:** charging failures, reduced range, and high-voltage system faults, some of which have been the subject of recalls
- **Water leaks and wind noise,** including in removable-roof Bronco models
- **Older PowerShift transmissions** in the Focus and Fiesta, which generated years of complaints and litigation

If your problem is not on this list, it can still qualify. What matters is whether a warranty defect substantially impairs the use, value, or safety of your vehicle and was not fixed.

## How Ford Handles Lemon Law Claims

Ford typically routes owners through its customer relationship center and its dealer network, and may offer goodwill repairs or extended coverage instead of a buyback. Those offers can sound helpful, but accepting one may make your claim harder later. Talk to a lawyer before signing anything Ford sends you.

California updated its lemon law procedures in 2025. Some manufacturers opted into the new process, which requires a written demand at least 30 days before filing suit and gives the manufacturer 30 days to make an offer. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I get a Ford buyback in California?
Yes, if your Ford has a warranty defect the dealer could not fix after a reasonable number of attempts. Under California's lemon law, Ford may have to repurchase or replace the vehicle and pay your attorney fees.

### My Ford dealer says it could not duplicate the problem. Do I still have a case?
Possibly. A "could not duplicate" repair order still documents that you reported the problem. Keep every repair order and record videos of the problem when it happens safely.

### Does lemon law cover Ford electric vehicles like the Mustang Mach-E and F-150 Lightning?
Yes. Electric vehicles are covered the same as gas vehicles, including problems with the high-voltage battery, charging system, and drive units.
