## What the Manufacturer Refunds in a Buyback

When a California lemon law case resolves as a repurchase, the manufacturer generally must pay:

- **Down payment and trade-in credit**
- **All monthly payments you made**
- **Payoff of the remaining loan or lease balance,** sent directly to your lender
- **Sales tax, registration, license, and other official fees**
- **Finance charges** you paid
- **Incidental costs,** such as towing and rental cars tied to the defect

The manufacturer can subtract a **usage offset** and certain non-manufacturer items, such as aftermarket add-ons you chose to buy.

## How the Mileage Offset Works

The offset is meant to account for the use you got from the vehicle before the problem started. It is generally calculated like this:

> (Miles when you first took the car in for the defect ÷ 120,000) × price paid for the vehicle

**Example:** You paid $48,000. The first repair visit for the defect was at 6,000 miles.
6,000 ÷ 120,000 = 0.05. 0.05 × $48,000 = **$2,400 offset.**

Miles you drove after the first repair visit generally do not increase the offset. That is one reason it matters to report the problem early and make sure the dealer writes it down.

## Replacement and Cash-and-Keep

- **Replacement:** a comparable new vehicle, with the manufacturer covering related taxes and fees, less the offset.
- **Cash and keep:** you keep the car and receive a cash payment. This works well when the problem is annoying but you like the vehicle.

## Civil Penalties

If the manufacturer **willfully** violated the law, for example by refusing a valid buyback demand without a good reason, a court can award a civil penalty of **up to two times your actual damages**. This is why many manufacturers settle valid claims.

## Attorney Fees

California's lemon law requires the manufacturer to pay your reasonable attorney fees and costs when you prevail. Those fees are paid on top of your recovery, not taken out of it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is the mileage offset calculated in a California lemon law buyback?
The offset is generally the number of miles on the vehicle when you first brought it in for the defect, divided by 120,000, multiplied by the price you paid for the vehicle.

### Do I get my down payment back?
Yes. In a repurchase, the manufacturer typically refunds your down payment and the monthly payments you made, and pays off the remaining loan or lease balance.

### What is a civil penalty in a lemon law case?
If the manufacturer willfully failed to comply with the lemon law, a court can award a civil penalty of up to two times your actual damages, on top of those damages.
